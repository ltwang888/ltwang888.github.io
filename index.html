📖 My Story

During a family lunch, the young son of one of my relatives handed me a 3×3 Rubik's Cube and smiled as he said,

"You can't go home until you solve the daisy cross!"

He patiently showed me how to make the daisy cross, the first step toward solving the white cross, but it still took me quite a few minutes before I finally completed it. When I succeeded, he smiled again and said,

"I'll challenge you again at our next family gathering."

He was serious.

I smiled and accepted the challenge.

🎯 My Challenge

After returning home, I began watching YouTube videos and learned the beginner's method. Before long, my curiosity led me to larger cubes, and I soon added a 4×4 and a 5×5 cube to continue the adventure.

Solving larger cubes was great fun. After a few weeks, however, I became interested in faster solving methods and began studying the beginner CFOP method for the 3×3 cube and the Yau method for the 4×4 cube. Eventually, I learned to solve the 3×3, 4×4, and 5×5 cubes comfortably using these methods, although it took weeks to memorize the 2-look OLL and 2-look PLL algorithms.

Then I began encountering some major obstacles.

I could not understand how OLL parity and PLL parity worked on the 4×4 cube. Even more frustrating, I found tutorials on the Yau 3-2-3 pairing method—especially the procedures for solving the Last Three Edge Pairs (L3E)—very difficult to understand. No matter how many videos I watched, I could not fully grasp the underlying logic.

Later, while solving the 5×5 cube, I encountered another challenge: the Last Two Centers (L2C). Once again, I wondered whether the many L2C cases could be understood more systematically, with fewer algorithms to memorize.

My curiosity eventually led me to the 6×6 cube, where the larger 4×4 center blocks made L2C appear even more complicated. This raised a natural question: could the same philosophy of reducing memorization and understanding local structures also be extended to the 6×6 cube?

🔍 My Journey

As an engineer, I decided to approach these problems differently.

Rather than memorizing more algorithms, I wanted to understand why they worked.

I began analyzing parity cases, L3E, and L2C configurations systematically. I drew diagrams, classified cases, counted moves, and searched for simpler and more systematic ways to understand the cube. What began as personal study notes gradually evolved into The Wang Method Series.

These notebooks were originally written to help me understand the logic behind solving the Rubik's Cube. Along the way, I realized that many of the explanations I had been searching for simply did not exist in one place, so I decided to organize my own understanding and share it with others.

💡 My Philosophy

I have great admiration for the many cubers who have developed elegant solving algorithms over the years. My goal is not to replace their work, but to better understand their algorithms and, hopefully, make them a little easier for beginners to learn.

I believe learning the Rubik's Cube should be logical, systematic, and enjoyable. Rather than asking beginners simply to memorize numerous algorithms, I hope these notebooks help readers understand why solutions work and discover simpler ways to approach unfamiliar cases.

If these notebooks help even one beginner or one young cuber better understand and enjoy the Rubik's Cube, I will feel that my effort has been worthwhile.

The complete book and individual notebooks are available below for free download. Additional notebooks may be added in the future.

Thank you for visiting my website.

I hope you enjoy exploring the wonderful world of the Rubik's Cube as much as I have.

Happy cubing!
